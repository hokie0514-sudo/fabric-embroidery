# Fabric and Embroidery Conversion Prompt

Convert the supplied flat image into a realistic, finely detailed fabric-and-embroidery artwork. Change materials only. Treat the source image as the sole authority for content and composition; do not redesign it.

Automatically distinguish the background from the foreground using visual meaning, boundaries, and layer relationships rather than color alone. Convert the background, margins, and negative space into fine plain-weave fabric while retaining their original colors. Convert all foreground text, graphics, logos, lines, borders, and decorative elements into embroidery. Preserve filled areas inside foreground graphics. Keep genuine cutouts, letter counters, and gaps open to their original underlying layers. Preserve existing transparency without adding a new background.

Use smooth, matte, tightly woven cotton or linen with subtle warp-and-weft detail and a consistent texture scale. Use fine thread and low-relief machine embroidery for the foreground, with restrained thread sheen, tiny fibers, and minimal contact shadows. Keep fabric weave clearly distinguishable from embroidery stitches.

Plan stitch direction automatically from each region's width, curvature, and boundaries. Fill broad continuous regions with closely spaced parallel rows of short stitches, staggered endpoints, and one stable dominant direction. Use fine satin stitches across narrow widths, or appropriately scaled short stitches, for slender strokes and fine lines. Change direction gradually around curves. Maintain consistent thread thickness, even density, and a smooth, low surface. Keep letter counters and narrow gaps clear; do not thicken or merge strokes.

Do not split a continuous color field into mirrored halves or add central seams, raised ridges, or repeated contour outlines. Avoid long floating threads spanning entire regions and stitches converging into knots. Unless these structures already exist in the source artwork, do not introduce herringbone, chevron, feather-like, braided, radial, or repeated V-shaped stitch patterns. Stitch texture must support the original shapes rather than become a new decorative pattern. Avoid thick yarn, puffed embroidery, plastic embossing, and conspicuous light-and-dark bands.

Preserve all original text, numbers, punctuation, capitalization, language, font characteristics, line breaks, relative type sizes, spacing, alignment, element positions, proportions, silhouettes, overlaps, margins, and colors. Elements that originally share a color must continue to match. Do not translate, rewrite, correct, add, or remove content. Preserve the original orientation and aspect ratio without cropping or stretching.

Present the complete artwork flat and front-facing under soft, even lighting. Keep the design clear at thumbnail size and the separate fabric and stitch textures visible at close range. Add no external scene, props, frame, perspective distortion, depth-of-field blur, or watermark.

Compare the result with the source to check text, layout, cutouts, and stitch direction. Save the result as a separate file and preserve the original.
